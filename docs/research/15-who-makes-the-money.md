# 15: Who makes the money on prediction markets

Researched 2026-10-10 for the main session's question (no brief file): who are the documented large winners on Polymarket and Kalshi (2022–2026), and what exactly is their edge?

## Answer in five lines

1. **Eight-figure winners are real but rare.** Polymarket's all-time leaderboard (2026-10-10) has only **5 wallets** at $10M or more (two are one person's) and **178** at $1M or more, out of roughly 2.5–3 million. About 70% of wallets lose, and the top 1% of profitable users take **76.5%** of all profits (588M trades). Kalshi publishes no per-account data.
2. **There is no single formula.** The big results came from four different things: one researched election bet with tens of millions at stake (Théo, **$47.85M** on four public accounts, over $85M by Chainalysis's count); sports trading at about **1¢ profit per share** on over a billion shares (swisstony, RN1; method undocumented); multi-million-dollar single-match bets (seven new World Cup accounts made $7–9M each; another lost **$11.6M**); and professional market making, paid in spread (the gap between buy and sell prices), rebates and even equity (Susquehanna, Jump). Arbitrage bots, crypto speed bots, weather traders and charged insiders made thousands to about $2M each.
3. **The money flows the same way in every dataset:** from takers (who accept a posted price, as our trader does) to makers (who post prices and wait), and from YES and long-shot buyers to NO and favourite buyers. Kalshi takers lost **1.12% per trade** before fees over 72.1M trades; Polymarket's winners are mostly limit-order liquidity providers.
4. **Speed edges are out of reach for us.** Arbitrage gaps (moments when contracts that must pay $1 cost less) that open and close within the hour last a median **16 seconds**; longer ones exist, but nobody has shown they pay after fees. Cross-venue sports gaps last about 9 seconds, and Polymarket holds taker orders for 150 ms (crypto) or about 1 s (live sports). The steadiest big winners net 0.5–1¢ per share; our roughly 8% cost is about 4¢ per share at 50¢. LLM agents trading at our cadence with real money lost **16–31%** on Kalshi in eight weeks (about 1% on Polymarket).
5. **The realistic goal is to lose less, not to copy the winners.** Stop taker bets where our forecasters add nothing and never buy long-shot YES; then test, offline first, buying NO in Kalshi mention and single-name markets, where YES at 50¢ settled YES only about **40%** of the time (35,000+ markets; gross of fees; under regulatory review). Sports against sharp lines, favourites near resolution and delayed wallet-copying are worth testing but likely break-even. Any code change needs Joey's approval.

## Findings

Grades: **strong** = reproducible exchange data, exchange docs, filings, peer review; **moderate** = large-sample working papers, major journalism, exchange statements; **weak** = vendor or single-analyst write-ups; **anecdotal** = crypto news, social media, self-reported profit, marketing. "Checked" = verified at source by the main session on 2026-10-10. Every leaderboard or news profit figure carries survivorship bias: equal-sized losers are rarely visible.

### 1. How concentrated the profits are

**1.1 Most wallets lose; a fraction of 1% of wallets take most of the profit.**

The Akey abstract gives no top-0.1% share and no share losing; those come from the dataset card and coverage ([GIGAZINE, 2026-05-25](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260525-polymarket-win-or-lose/): 68.8% lost; about 1,200 accounts hold over half of profits). [Galaxy Research](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2294339) (2026-10-01, 3.07M wallets; weak) finds 69.2% of retail accounts below break-even. Strength: **moderate**; all methods agree on direction.

**1.2 Eight figures in one wallet is a club of about four people.** One person or firm can spread profit over several wallets, as Théo did, so a few more may exist unseen. Leaderboard, 2026-10-10: 5 wallets at $10M+, 178 at $1M+ ($516M together), 1,874 at $100k+ ($981M); rank 100 has $1.59M, rank 1,000 $0.20M. The API spec calls the all-time board "the realized-only lifetime ledger", with volume "in shares, not USDC" ([Data API](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v2/leaderboard?time_period=all&category=overall&sort_by=PNL&limit=1000)). Strength: **strong**. Survivorship: only winners appear; the largest loser is at −$21.8M ([CrowdIntel](https://crowdintel.xyz/blog/only-2-percent-of-polymarket-traders-made-1000), 2026-06-01; weak), and over 100,000 accounts lost $1,000+ from January 2025 to April 2026 ([Bloomberg via Yahoo, 2026-04-28](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/100-000-polymarket-accounts-booked-062234759.html)).

**1.3 The top is sports and 2024-election politics.** Of the top 100, 67 wallets earn mostly in sports (64.7% of top-100 profit), 25 in politics (30.3%), 3 in crypto (1.5%); 45 earn under 5¢ per share traded, a high-turnover profile that fits market making. Other categories' all-time leaders are small: crypto $2.22M, economics $1.60M, weather $0.32M, mentions $0.29M. Strength: **strong** for counts; categories are proxies, not strategies.

**1.4 Kalshi has no account-level data.** Kalshi told the WSJ losers outnumbered winners 2.9 to 1 in one month (May 2026; WSJ coverage in 1.1). Its opt-in leaderboard is topped by "Blackbriar" at 8.24 million "projected PnL" units, units undocumented ([API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/v1/social/leaderboard?metric_name=projected_pnl&time_period=all_time&limit=25)). Strength: **moderate** (Kalshi chose the window); **strong** for the board.

**1.5 Two widely quoted figures are wrong.** "Only 0.51% of wallets made over $1,000" has no primary source (reposts return HTTP 410); datable late-2024 analyses show about 1.2% ([Cointelegraph, 2024-10-09](https://cointelegraph.com/news/gambling-on-polymarket-profitability-data-revealed)), 2026 studies 2–2.4%. DeFi Oasis ([Yahoo, 2025-12-29](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/70-polymarket-traders-lost-money-192327162.html)) claimed 668 wallets above $1M and $3.7B for the top 0.04%, but the leaderboard shows 178 at $1M+ and other estimates of total top profit are $0.5–1.0B: probably a method error (my guess: market makers' split-and-merge flows counted as profit). Trust the leaderboard. Strength: **strong** that they conflict.

**1.6 Wallets aren't people, and lifetime totals mislead.** About 25% of Polymarket volume over three years looked like wash trading (trading with yourself), 45% in sports ([Columbia via Decrypt, 2025-11-07](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading); moderate). Akey et al. say persistence "may represent sample selection rather than skill".

### 2. The named winners and what their edge was

**2.1 Théo: one researched, capital-heavy election bet.** A self-described French ex-banker ran Fredi9999, Theo4, PrincessCaro and Michie. He bought Trump for the popular vote at 32–37¢ (about 40% of the profit) and the presidency at 54–60¢ ([Theo4 positions](https://data-api.polymarket.com/closed-positions?user=0x56687bf447db6ffa42ffe2204a05edaa20f55839&limit=3&sortBy=REALIZEDPNL&sortDirection=DESC); strong). About $30M went in before he commissioned YouGov to ask swing-state voters whom their neighbours would vote for. After seeing the results he raised the stake to about $80M across 11 accounts ([The Free Press, 2024-11-26](https://www.thefp.com/p/french-whale-makes-85-million-on-polymarket-trump-win); moderate). The Free Press dates that to the end of October 2024; Polymarket's CEO said "about three weeks before Election Day" (CBS 60 Minutes, 2025-11-30; an interested source). He was about $3M down the day before the vote (Business Insider; moderate). Edge: **information plus capital and conviction**; one event can't separate luck from skill, and the poll numbers were never published.
Conflict reconciled: **$47.85M** is the four public accounts (leaderboard 2026-10-10, flat since 2024-11-28; strong); **more than $85M** is Chainalysis's estimate for up to 11 accounts linked by shared cash-out exchanges ([Bloomberg, 2024-11-07](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/new-analysis-boosts-trump-whales-polymarket-haul-to-85-million); moderate). A $78.7M figure credited to Chainalysis couldn't be traced. The gap is account scope: trust $47.85M as the floor and about $85M for the whole cluster.

**2.2 High-turnover sports accounts made $13–24M at about 1¢ per share; method undocumented.** swisstony: $18.42M on 1.85B shares since August 2025; RN1: $12.86M on 1.35B since July 2025 ([leaderboard](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v1/leaderboard?timePeriod=ALL&orderBy=PNL&limit=50); strong); reported win rates 53–55% over 127,000–200,000 positions (Deadspin, 2026-05-29; weak). swisstony's top realized wins ($0.8–1.2M each) were NO bets against World Cup favourites. kch123 ($11.35M on 293M shares) made seven-figure single-match bets (strong). Edge: probably automated pricing and execution at scale, possibly market making; **not verified**. A 53–55% win rate means little by itself, because it depends on the prices paid; profit of about 1¢ per share over more than a billion shares is the telling number. Survivorship: suntori, among the five highest-volume accounts ever, is down $8–11M.
Conflict reconciled: Polymarket's daily PnL history shows swisstony at $23.63M and RN1 at $14.15M ([series](https://user-pnl-api.polymarket.com/user-pnl?user_address=0x204f72f35326db932158cba6adff0b9a1da95e14&interval=all&fidelity=1d)), up to about 30% above the leaderboard for active accounts and identical for dormant ones. The leaderboard is documented as realized-only; the series is undocumented (probably includes open positions, my inference). Use the leaderboard; treat active-account figures as ±30%.

**2.3 Seven new accounts won $7–9M each at the 2026 World Cup; another lost $11.6M.** mintblade, fishalive, frostrizz and four others made $57.5M together (18% of the top-50 pool), each staking $5–7M on one roughly 50¢ match, then going quiet. coldsway lost $11.6M in 10 days on the same kind of bet, and DEEDDIT was $10.8M down before finishing +$8.05M (PnL series; strong). Edge: **capital and conviction, or private information**; nothing public tells them apart. Survivorship bias in its plainest form.

**2.4 A full-time professional makes low millions over years.** Domer, an ex-poker player trading since 2007 ([DL News, 2024-06-21](https://dlnews.com/articles/markets/meet-the-most-prolific-bettor-on-polymarket/); anecdotal), trades as ImJustKen (Benzinga; weak): $2.27M on 497M shares, about 0.5¢ per share; his best trade, Warsh for Fed chair at about 4¢, made $1.34M (strong).

**2.5 Crypto speed bots made $0.8–2.4M each, then stalled.** 0x8dxd rose from $314 (2025-12-05) to $2.38M (2026-04-29), flat since; gabagool22 (about $0.87M) and distinct-baguette (about $0.81M) flattened by March 2026 (PnL series; strong). Coverage says 0x8dxd bought the side of 15-minute BTC, ETH or SOL markets that Binance and Coinbase prices had already decided, before Polymarket repriced, winning about 98% of bets ([Finbold](https://finbold.com/trading-bot-turns-313-into-438000-on-polymarket-in-a-month/), January 2026; anecdotal). Edge: **speed**. They are survivors: 63% of wallets in these markets lost in one measured week (4.4).

**2.6 Weather traders made tens to hundreds of thousands.** The all-time weather leader, gopfan2, made $317k and the top ten $80–317k each ([weather board](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v1/leaderboard?category=WEATHER&timePeriod=ALL&orderBy=PNL&limit=25); strong). The biggest weather payoff was suspected sensor tampering: $119 became $21,398 on a Paris airport spike on 2026-04-15 ([NPR, 2026-04-23](https://www.wypr.org/2026-04-23/french-police-probe-suspected-weather-device-tampering-after-odd-polymarket-bet); moderate). Report 07 agrees.

**2.7 Inside information: frequent, small, now prosecuted.** A US Army Special Forces soldier allegedly bought 436,000 YES shares at about 7.4¢ on Maduro's exit before the 2026-01-03 raid and realized over $404,000; indicted on 2026-04-23 with a CFTC case, the first US criminal charges over prediction-market bets ([NPR via TPR](https://www.tpr.org/2026-04-23/u-s-soldier-charged-with-suspected-polymarket-insider-trading-over-maduro-raid)). A Google employee ("AlphaRaccoon") allegedly made about $1.2M on Year in Search markets ([CFTC 9237-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9237-26), 2026-05-27). Two Israelis were indicted over $162,663 made on the June 2025 Iran strikes; Kalshi fined a MrBeast editor about $20,000. No pleas or verdicts yet. With unresolved clusters (about $1M in six fresh wallets before the 2026-02-28 Iran strikes; the April 2026 ceasefire; the 2025 Nobel), detected cases total about **$4–5M**, about 1.5% of the top-50 pool. A screen of 93,000+ markets flags about **$143M** of "anomalous" profit, February 2024–February 2026, as a lower bound ([Mitts & Ofir, 2026-03-25](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/03/25/from-iran-to-taylor-swift-informed-trading-in-prediction-markets/); moderate), and Akey et al. find insiders "unlikely to explain the performance of the largest winners". Strength: **strong** for charges as filed, **moderate** for totals.

**2.8 Where the top 50's $311M came from (judgment from leaderboard arithmetic).** About 45–55% from capital and conviction on single events, 25–30% from high-turnover, probably automated trading, perhaps 6–10% from private research (Théo's polls), 1–2% from detected insiders and very little from speed bots; liquidity provision can't be separated out. The nearest thing to "mathematicians" is quant firms (Susquehanna, Jump) and anonymous bots, none of which publishes profits. No documented eight-figure winner took liquidity every 30 minutes with forecasts weaker than the market.

### 3. Market makers: who they are, how they earn, what it takes

A market maker posts a buy price (bid) and a sell price (ask), earns the spread plus venue payments, and risks being "picked off": filled at a stale price just after news.

**3.1 Few market makers are named.** Susquehanna (SIG) is Kalshi's only announced external market maker (2024-04-03; targets of 2–3¢ spreads and 100,000+ contracts of depth; [Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/liquid-prediction-markets-are-finally-here); moderate). Kalshi's in-house affiliate exists for "profitability" (CFTC rulebook; strong) but was "not profitable" in late 2025, per a co-founder (anecdotal). Jump Trading reportedly takes equity in Kalshi and Polymarket for liquidity, with about 20 traders ([Decrypt on Bloomberg, 2026-02-09](https://decrypt.co/357519/jump-trading-earn-stakes-polymarket-kalshi); moderate, unconfirmed). Polymarket names none.

**3.2 Kalshi pays makers in layers, mostly confidential.** Designated market makers get "predetermined incentives", fee cuts, higher position limits and risk tools under private agreements, plus an auction-priced program ([rulebook, 2024-02-26](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/filings/orgrules/24/02/rules02262412176.pdf); strong). Any US member can join the **Liquidity Incentive Program**: $1–1,000 per market per day for resting orders near the best price, scored every second, only when both sides hold the target size (usually 1,000 contracts), halving per tick behind ([Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program); strong). Configured pools grew from $218k (Oct 2025) to **$6.24M (Sep 2026)**, a median of $17 per market per day ([API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/incentive_programs?status=active&limit=10000); strong; payouts lower and unpublished).

**3.3 Polymarket makers stack spread, rewards and rebates.** Liquidity rewards: $201k a day over 18,863 markets on 2026-10-10 (median $3 each; 729 hold half), scored each minute by squared closeness to the midpoint ([sampling-markets](https://clob.polymarket.com/sampling-markets); strong). Maker rebates (checked, [docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/maker-rebates)): makers pay no fee and get a share of taker fees (crypto 20%, sports 15%, finance, politics, economics, culture, weather, other, mentions and tech 25%, geopolitics fee-free), paid daily in proportion to each maker's fee-equivalent in the same market, $1 minimum. Taker rates: crypto 0.07; sports, economics, culture, weather, other 0.05; finance, politics, mentions, tech 0.04; geopolitics 0. A rebate is 0.19–0.35¢ per share at 50¢; Polymarket US pays makers 0.31¢ per contract (CFTC filing; strong).
**Correction to report 10:** its F6 called a 2026-07-10 sports taker-rate rise (0.03 to 0.05) refuted. Polymarket's [changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog) has it, dated 2026-07-10, along with a sports maker-rebate cut from 25% to 15%. Report 10 still saw 0.03 sports markets, so the default probably changed with per-market exceptions. Strength: **strong**.

**3.4 What it takes is fast cancelling.** Polymarket runs in AWS London with co-location after KYC, and cancels all of a user's orders after 10 s without a "heartbeat" ([docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock.md); strong); Kalshi offers FIX, WebSocket feeds, auto-cancelling order groups and AWS PrivateLink (docs; strong). Taker delays (150 ms crypto, about 1 s live sports) protect only makers who can cancel inside them; Jump's models lean on "real-time data … on the field" (Bloomberg, July 2026; moderate).

**3.5 A slow maker mostly collects bad fills.** In a 393-market sample (2026-10-10), 28% of Polymarket reward markets had no qualifying quotes but a median pool of $3 a day, and prices moved 2¢ or more in 7.3% of 30-minute windows in the median market (24% in game markets) (one day; weak). One 2–5¢ adverse fill on 100 shares erases days of such a pool. The best-documented small bot, "poly-maker", made about $200 a day on $10,000 ($700–800 at the 2024 peak) until rewards were cut ([Polymarket newsletter, 2025-05-28](https://news.polymarket.com/p/automated-market-making-on-polymarket); anecdotal, a survivor). Our own resting-order test returned −16% to −31% (CHECKS.md, report 11).

### 4. Arbitrage and latency

**Arbitrage** is buying a set of contracts that must pay more than it costs whatever happens, such as YES plus NO for under $1. **Latency** is delay; latency trading means acting on news before the price moves.

**4.1 About $40M of Polymarket arbitrage in one fee-free year, spread thin.** Markets resolved April 2024–April 2025: $39.6M in all; the top account made $2.01M over 4,049 transactions, the top ten $8.18M; about 1% of US-election opportunities were taken ([Saguillo et al., AFT 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)). No fees were deducted, prices were carried forward up to 2.5 hours, and the lead author calls it "an upper bound on extractable profit". Strength: **strong** as a measurement, **moderate** as money pocketed. No arbitrageur is documented at eight figures (report 02).

**4.2 In 2026, gaps last seconds and profit per trade has collapsed** (checked). [Gebele, Mutzel & Matthes](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00666) (arXiv, 2026-08-01) find $1.12M of arbitrage profit across two channels. In an order-book sample from 2026-04-14 to 2026-05-19, the median gap lasted **16.15 s** on the YES side (253 episodes) and **7.99 s** on the NO side (5). But 366 of 624 episodes (58.7%) spanned the whole sampled hour, lasting at least about 50 minutes, and were left out of the median: longer-lived violations exist, though the paper doesn't show they were tradable at size after fees. Median profit per conversion fell from about $1 (to July 2024) to $0.20 (late 2024–2025) and $0.08 (early 2026). This updates report 02's plain "about 16 seconds". Strength: **moderate**.

**4.3 Cross-venue gaps exist; verified profit from them doesn't.** Same-event mid prices sit 2–4% from parity, about 3¢ (up to 7¢) in the 2024 Kalshi–Polymarket election market, mostly because one venue prices higher or the rules differ; zero fees were assumed ([Gebele & Matthes, arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706), Jan 2026; 102,275 events, 2018–2025; moderate). A developer saw 870 sports gaps in a day (median life about 9 s; 96% gone in 30 s) and netted $439 on a match with $20.8M traded ([dev.to](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf), July 2026; anecdotal). Two 2026 taker fees cost 2.75–3.5¢ per $1 pair at 50¢ before spreads (our arithmetic).

**4.4 Short-dated crypto is a millisecond race.** In one week of 15-minute crypto markets (2026-02-02 to 02-08; 15.3M fills, 46,945 wallets), makers netted **+$728,501** and takers lost the same before fees, despite winning 53% of fills; 36.8% of wallets profited ([Telonex, 2026-02-13](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m); weak). Taker fees there began on **2026-01-05** (changelog; The Block reported 01-06 and Dune dates 01-07; use the changelog), now 1.75¢ per share at 50¢; settlement has used a 60-second Chainlink average since August 2026 (strong).
Conflict reconciled, crypto delay: a 500 ms delay was reportedly removed around 2026-02-18 ([Protos, 2026-02-20](https://protos.com/polymarket-ends-trading-loophole-for-bitcoin-quants/); anecdotal, not in the changelog); a docs page says 250 ms; the changelog says **50 ms from 2026-08-17** and **150 ms from 2026-09-04**. Trust the changelog: 150 ms.

**4.5 Live-sports latency is a data-feed race the venues are closing.** Polymarket cancels sports limit orders at game start and delays marketable ones ([Help Center](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders); strong); 173 NBA games produced only 7 single-market arbitrage episodes during play (median life 3.6 s), plus 290 combinatorial ones ([Cheng, Yang & Zou, 2026-04-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864); moderate); both venues are buying official low-latency data. An e-sports account's reported $208k in a quarter is anecdotal.
Conflict reconciled, sports delay: the Help Center (2026-04-20) says 3 s, with 1 s tested on NBA and MLB; the live API (2026-10-10) shows 1 s on 876 reward markets, 3 s on 3 and none on 17,984, and 1 s on 321 of 1,147 upcoming games (strong). Trust the live data: mostly 1 s.

**4.6 What it takes:** servers near London or Ohio, live order-book feeds, sub-second loops, funded accounts on both venues, capital locked until resolution and legal access (US persons only via Polymarket US). A 30-minute cycle is about 100 times slower than the median arbitrage gap (16 s), and over 10,000 times slower than the crypto speed race (150 ms) (my arithmetic). Strength: **moderate** (Polymarket docs; a vendor for Kalshi's Ohio servers).

### 5. Makers versus takers: where the money flows

A **taker** trades at once against a price someone else posted; buying at the ask, our trader is a taker. A **maker** posted that price and waited.

**5.1 Kalshi takers lose and makers gain, most of all on cheap YES** (checked). Becker, Kalshi only, 72.1M trades, $18.26B, June 2021–November 2025 ([web version 2026-01-18](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure); SSRN 2026-08-01): per trade, before fees, takers −1.12% and makers +1.12%; dollar-weighted, YES buyers −1.02% and NO buyers +0.83%; takers lost at 80 of 99 prices. The gap was 0.17 points in finance, 1.02 in politics, 2.2–2.7 in sports, weather and crypto, and 4.8–7.3 in entertainment, media and world events. Strength: **moderate** (huge public dataset; single author, not peer reviewed).

**5.2 The maker edge appeared only in 2024** (checked; Becker; moderate). Through 2023, Kalshi takers averaged +2.0% per trade. The gap reversed from 2024 Q2 and widened after the election, a 5.3-point swing, as quarterly volume went from $30M to $820M. My inference: professional makers (SIG from April 2024) and less-informed takers arrived together, so the maker edge depends on who is on the other side.

**5.3 Two Kalshi studies, one direction.** Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (2021 to April 2025; 46,282 contracts, 313,972 prices; [paper](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)): makers −9.64% and takers −31.46% after takers' fees; takers bought 56.5% of contracts at 10¢ or less; makers buying at 50¢+ made +2.6% with a 33% standard deviation. Strength: **moderate**.
Conflict reconciled: Bürgi weights each contract equally, so cheap long shots that go to zero dominate; Becker weights each trade, before fees. Different weighting, same direction: use Bürgi for the per-bet damage on cheap contracts and Becker for the typical transfer per trade.

**5.4 Polymarket's winners mostly provide liquidity** (checked). Akey et al. (moderate): successful traders mostly post limit orders, unsuccessful ones mostly take liquidity with market orders. Long shots bought by accepting an offer lost 22% before fees (dollar-weighted), against 18% for posted orders ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1), Sept 2026; 560.9M purchases; moderate). The top 1% of maker addresses supply 84.1% of maker volume (Qin & Yang, June 2026, full on-chain archive; moderate). In 222M trades, bots earned $133M with 49.9% directional accuracy while retail, right 51.3% of the time, lost by entering later at worse prices (Della Vedova, [SSRN 6191618](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6191618), 2026; moderate).

**5.5 Skill sits in a few percent of accounts, mostly not the top earners.** Of 1.72M accounts (2023–2025), 3.14% test as skilled; skilled traders plus market makers, under 3.5% of accounts, take over 30% of gains. 44% of the skilled stay skilled out of sample (about 10% for mutual funds), but only 12% of top earners are skilled and about 60% of "lucky winners" turn into losers (Gómez-Cram et al., via [The Block, 2026-04-26](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study); moderate study, numbers from summaries).

**5.6 The flow is 1–2% of volume and lands on a few thousand accounts.** The Roosevelt Institute estimates Kalshi takers lost $583.5M from July 2021 to May 2026, $371.6M in sports ([2026-07-07](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/blog/since-kalshis-launch-ordinary-users-have-lost-half-a-billion-dollars/); weak); Kalshi replies that maker and taker are order-book labels, not kinds of people, and that 3–4% interest is left out ([2026-07-10](https://news.kalshi.com/p/no-ordinary-users-arent-losing-half-a-billion-dollars-on-kalshi)). On Betfair soccer (200,000+ matches, 2022–2024), takers lost 2.5% and makers made 0.6% after a 2% commission ([Whelan, 2026-01-26](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Betfair.pdf); moderate). My judgment: slow takers without an information edge fund this flow, and that is our profile (forecaster weight about 0, λ about 0; CHECKS.md).

### 6. What is documented for slower participants

**6.1 The biggest bias a slow taker can reach: YES optimism in Kalshi mention and single-name markets.** Mention markets pay on whether a speaker says a given word. Across 35,000+ of them, YES at 50% settled YES about **40%** of the time, and buying YES at the first price seen lost about 11% (WSJ 2026, cited by [Bartlett & O'Hara, 2026-07-02](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf); moderate). In single-name markets (one company or person; 41.6M trades), YES overpricing grew from about zero in 2023–24 to **+10 to +18 points** by early 2026; contracts priced in the forties settled YES 37% of the time. Selling YES with resting orders made $9.91M gross over 14,988 markets, with three times the maker book's dispersion (same paper; moderate). All gross of fees and partly maker-side. My arithmetic: NO at 50¢ with YES winning 40% is worth about +10¢ (+20%) before costs. Risks: a wording trap moved a Netflix "Warner Bros" market from about 90¢ to under 40¢ in 40 minutes; a White House teleprompter operator is suspected of trading on speeches ([NPR, 2026-07-16](https://www.wunc.org/2026-07-16/officials-probe-whether-white-house-teleprompter-operator-profited-off-trumps-words)); the CFTC opened a review and Kalshi pulled mention markets from sports in August 2026 ([NPR, 2026-08-13](https://www.kuow.org/npr-news/2026-08-13/feds-probe-mention-markets-amid-white-house-kalshi-controversy); moderate). A 30-minute poller can act only before the event.

**6.2 Favourites near resolution ("bond" buying): break-even after costs, with fat tails.** Polymarket buys at 90¢+ earned about 0.83¢ per dollar before fees, nothing in sports, and the heaviest favourite buyers did worse (Cardozo; moderate); Kalshi takers made about +0.6% after fees at 90–99¢ (report 01). Costs at 95¢ (0.33¢ Kalshi, 0.24¢ Polymarket, plus half the spread) match the edge. Four big contested resolutions came within a year: Ukraine minerals (March 2025; a 91% favourite paid zero), the Zelensky suit ($237M+ traded), the Venezuela "invasion" and Kalshi's Khamenei death clause (Kalshi refunded losses) ([The Block, 2025-03-26](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented); [Decrypt, 2025-07-09](https://decrypt.co/329210/polymarket-rules-no-237m-bet-zelenskyy-suit?amp=1); moderate). Bought at 95–99¢, one such loss wipes out the profit of 19 to 99 wins; "1,800% annualized" bonding claims are marketing. See reports 01 and 03.
Conflict reconciled, holding rewards: announced at 4% (2025-09-24) and still 4.00% on a docs page; the Help Center (2026-06-01) says **3.25%** on 18–19 long-dated events ([Help Center](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364459-holding-rewards); strong). Trust the dated Help Center: 3.25%, variable, about 0.27% a month.

**6.3 Model pricing of niche markets: the markets already match public models.** Kalshi's economic markets are never significantly worse than the Bloomberg consensus, and the modal fed funds outcome has matched every decision since 2022 ([FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf); moderate; report 08). Kalshi's implied temperature beat the NBM forecast (2.44°F vs 2.70°F error) and candid weather bots found no edge (report 07; weak). Polymarket crypto thresholds sat 5.6–11 points above option-implied odds (half-life about 4 hours), but on 2023 data and 16 simulated trades ([Portnaya, 2026-06-17](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19517); weak); a 2026 study found Polymarket's daily BTC brackets beat an options model (weak).

**6.4 Sports against sharp books: beating the close is necessary, not sufficient.** **Closing-line value (CLV)** compares your price with the last price before the game; to **de-vig** is to strip out the bookmaker's margin so both sides sum to 100%. In 3,670 NBA games, three of five positive-CLV deciles still lost money; only the top decile (about 11% CLV) clearly beat the roughly 4.5% margin ([Whelan, 2026-08-12](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595); weak). There is no CLV track record on Kalshi or Polymarket, Kalshi's liquid games carry 1–2¢ margins, and our own favourite-at-the-ask test lost 1.6% ± 2.6% over 606 markets (report 09).

**6.5 Copying wallets: untested.** Only 12% of top earners are skilled (5.5), so copying by profit mostly copies luck. On a public-ledger futures exchange the best wallets stay informative (rank correlation 0.52), but the value shows at one-second horizons ([Zhai, August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373); moderate for that venue). Copy-bot vendors say late fills are 5–10% worse and publish no audited results (anecdotal). Kalshi hides trader identities, so this is Polymarket-only.

**6.6 LLM agents at our cadence lost money with real capital** (checked). Prediction Arena ([arXiv 2604.07355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07355), 2026-03-28): six models with $10,000 each, live 2026-01-12 to 2026-03-09 (57 days), deciding every 15–45 minutes. All six lost 16.0% to 30.8% on Kalshi (average −22.6%) and averaged −1.1% on Polymarket; one later model made +6.02% in a 3-day paper run. Prophet Arena's models, shown prices, also lost (best 0.943 back per unit staked). Strength: **moderate**; the closest public analogue to our trader.

## What a small automated paper trader could realistically do

Ranked by how likely each is to pay after our costs and how honestly we can test it. Only the mention-market bias has been measured as larger than our costs, and that measurement is before fees.

1. **Lose less first.** *Do:* no taker bets where our forecasters add nothing beyond the price; never buy long-shot YES. *Evidence:* no-information takers lose in every dataset (section 5; moderate); losses concentrate in cheap YES (5.1, report 01); our forecasters' weight and λ are about 0 (CHECKS.md); Prediction Arena's agents lost 16–31% on Kalshi (6.6; moderate). *Expected effect:* most of the 8% drag on no-edge bets goes, along with most bets. *How sure:* high. *Code change?* It may fit the betting rules (main session to check); any code change needs Joey's approval.
2. **Test fading YES optimism (buying NO) in Kalshi mention and single-name markets.** *Evidence:* 6.1; moderate, gross of fees, in-sample, partly maker-side. *Expected effect:* a few percent per bet or more if the gap survives fees and the ask (+20% gross at 50¢). *How sure:* low to moderate; supply is shrinking (CFTC review) and wording traps are costly. *Code change?* None to test offline; trading it is a new strategy that may need code (Joey's approval).
3. **Sports against de-vigged sharp-book prices, scored by CLV.** *Evidence:* 6.4 and report 09 (weak); data costs about $30 a month (The Odds API). *Expected effect:* about zero on liquid markets. *How sure:* moderate. *Code change?* The backtest needs only the data purchase (Joey's call); a live version needs code and approval.
4. **Favourites near resolution, plus holding rewards.** *Evidence:* 6.2; moderate. *Expected effect:* about break-even after costs, with occasional large dispute losses; the 3.25% reward covers only 18–19 events. *How sure:* moderate. *Code change?* Testable on our snapshots now; trading it is probably a betting-rule filter (main session to confirm).
5. **Copy skilled wallets with a delay.** *Evidence:* 6.5 (moderate for skill, anecdotal for copying); most top earners aren't skilled, and skilled traders are mostly makers, whose edge doesn't pass to a late taker. *Expected effect:* unknown, probably negative after 30 minutes. *How sure:* low. *Code change?* None for an offline backtest; a live version needs code and approval.
6. **Slow resting (maker) orders.** *Evidence:* 3.5 and our own test (−16% to −31%); simulate only with report 11's trade-through fill rule. *Expected effect:* negative, except perhaps in slow, long-dated markets. *How sure:* moderate. *Code change?* Yes, report 11's fill model, for Joey to approve.

**Not realistic for us:** market making at scale (capital, sub-second cancelling, private agreements); reward farming (scores decay between polls, stale quotes get picked off); both kinds of arbitrage (gaps last seconds; two fees exceed most of them); latency trading (150 ms to 1 s speed bumps, official data feeds); inside information (illegal, prosecuted); Théo-type single bets (one event, tens of millions at risk, losers of the same size).

## How to test it on our history

About 1,100 finished markets with 30-minute snapshots, forecasts and outcomes. Use report 01's rules: spread 10¢ or less, each market as two purchases (YES at the YES ask, NO at the NO ask), the real fee curve, an event-level bootstrap, and no conclusion from under about 200 purchases.

**T1. NO against YES at the ask, after full costs, by category.** First count our Kalshi mention markets (tickers containing MENTION, such as KXEARNINGSMENTIONNFLX) and single-name markets. *Confirms item 2:* there, NO is positive after fees by more than 2 standard errors and YES is negative. *Kills it:* NO within its ± of zero. Under 200 purchases means our history can't answer and a Kalshi trade download is needed. Extends report 01 T1–T2.

**T2. Favourites at 90¢+ at the ask, after fees, listing every blow-up.** Report n, wins against the implied rate, return after fees, and each 90¢+ loss with its cost. *Confirms break-even:* return within its ± of zero. *Worth a pre-registered challenger only if:* positive by more than twice its ± with every blow-up counted. See report 01 T4 and report 03 test 4.

**T3. Cross-venue gaps that last.** For matched Kalshi–Polymarket pairs (report 02 T4), count gaps larger than both taker fees plus both spreads in two consecutive 30-minute snapshots, then hand-check each for rule differences. *Expected:* rare, mostly rule differences. *Kills arbitrage for good:* under 1% of pair-snapshots, or mostly rule mismatches. A pass supports only a twin-price check (report 02), not arbitrage.

**T4. Delayed wallet copying (public Polymarket data, not our history).** Pick wallets using only data before a cut-off, once by leaderboard profit and once by a skill rule; replay their buys 30 minutes later at that moment's ask, with fees. *Confirms item 5:* positive after costs by more than 2 standard errors on 200+ out-of-sample trades. *Kills it:* zero or below, or no better than random wallets.

Already specified elsewhere: the sharp-book backtest (report 09, test 3), resting orders under the trade-through rule (report 11, T2) and the "lose less" filters (report 01, T5–T6).
