## Question

How should a paper trader simulate order fills (market and limit orders, and bets bigger than the top of the book) so its results match what real trading would get, given what the public APIs provide?

## What we already know

- Today every bet fills completely at the best ask plus 1¢, however little is offered there (BACKLOG B15).
- We store the best asks about every 30 minutes, with no depth or trade history.
- Our test: resting orders at the mid, counted as filled only when a later snapshot's ask reached our price, did worse than buying at the ask (−16% to −31%, against about −13%), because orders fill when the news turns against them.

## Find out

- What Kalshi's and Polymarket's public APIs offer: order-book depth, trade history, rate limits, and whether historical data can be downloaded.
- Standard practice for backtesting limit orders: queue position, fill-probability models, adverse selection, latency.
- How to size a bet against the displayed depth, and a slippage model for walking the book.
- Whether storing depth or trades each cycle is worth it, and what it would cost in storage.

## Leave out

Fees (brief 10); sizing policy (12).
